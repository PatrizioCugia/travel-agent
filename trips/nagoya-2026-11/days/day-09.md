A minshuku breakfast at [[maruya-tsumago]] — grilled fish, miso soup, pickles, rice — then the local bus over to Magome to start the hike from the higher end. Downhill is the kinder direction.

## The hike

The [[nakasendo-trail]] between Magome and Tsumago is the best-preserved stretch of the Edo-era highway that once linked Kyoto and Edo. Eight kilometres, roughly three hours, through cedar forest, past waterfalls and bear bells you ring yourself at trailside posts. Stone paving in places, packed earth in others. Travellers, daimyo processions, post-runners — for two centuries this was the route. In November the maples along the trail are well into red.

## Afternoon

Arrive into Tsumago by early afternoon. The village itself is the museum: power lines buried, signs hidden, wooden facades preserved by a 1968 community pact that refused to sell, lend, or destroy. Walk the main street to [[waki-honjin-okuya]] — the 1877 secondary inn for travelling lords — and [[tsumago-honjin]], the reconstructed main inn. If energy allows, the thirty-minute walk south to [[otsumago]] is worth it: smaller, even quieter, almost no other visitors.

## Evening

Back to [[maruya-tsumago]] for the cap. Dinner in the irori room, sake warm, the contrast with Nagoya complete.
