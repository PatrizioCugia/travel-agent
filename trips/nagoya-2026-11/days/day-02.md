## Morning

Subway south to [[atsuta-jingu]] before the crowds. The shrine is one of the deepest in Japan — the Kusanagi sword lives here — and walking the cedar approach in early light is the kind of slow, sensory entry that sets the tone for the whole week. Buy the omamori here, the count works out to five across the trip.

## Brunch

Cross the road to [[atsuta-houraiken-honten]] — the 1873 inventor of hitsumabushi. Walk-in only, expect a wait. Three quarters of eel, three ways: as is, with negi and wasabi, then drowned in dashi. If the main shop line is brutal, fall back to [[atsuta-houraiken-jinguten]] nearer the shrine.

## Afternoon

Train north for the Tokugawa half of the day. [[tokugawa-en]] is a true Edo garden at its November peak — maples already turning, the pond catching late light. Slip into [[tokugawa-art-museum]] for the Owari family treasures and pause at [[ryoguchiya-tokugawa]] inside for a wagashi and matcha at the 1634 counter. Walk it off through [[shirakabe-samurai-district]], white-walled and quiet.

## Dinner

[[hamasho-nishiki]] for Nagoya Cochin yakitori, tebasaki, doteni — the full izakaya register on the city's signature chicken.

## Night

End at [[bar-barns]] in Fushimi. Hirai-san behind the stick, low light, one Japanese whisky, one cocktail, no rush.
