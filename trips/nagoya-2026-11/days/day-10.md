title: Wide View Hida along the Kiso — into the Alps
---

Last breakfast at [[maruya-tsumago]]. The innkeepers send me off in the same quiet way they received me two evenings ago — a small bow at the door, the kind that doesn't ask for anything back. I take the local bus down the valley to [[nakatsugawa-station]], then change for the Wide View Hida, the limited express that traces the Kiso River north toward the mountains.

This is a ride people take for its own sake. Big panoramic windows on both sides, the carriage tilting through gorges the train was named for. I unwrap the Cochin chicken bento somewhere around Gero — the bird from a few days ago in Nagoya now slow-cooked into rice, soy, a little dashi. Eating it while pine forest and white water blur past feels like a quiet bookend to the urban week.

Off at [[takayama-station]] mid-afternoon. I don't linger — Takayama proper is for the way back. Straight to the Nohi bus heading up into Okuhida, climbing switchbacks through villages no train reaches. By the time I get off at Shin-Hirayu, the air has changed: thinner, colder, smelling of cedar and sulfur.

[[yamano-iori-hotaka-so]] is a small wooden building under tall pines. The okami shows me to my room, then to the rotenburo — outdoor bath, river noise, steam rising into the dusk. Dinner is hoba-miso grilled at the table, mountain vegetables, sansai I can't name. After two days of Edo, the register shifts again: I'm in the Alps now.
