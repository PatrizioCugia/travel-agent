title: Shinhotaka Ropeway and an afternoon of onsen
---

I aim for the first cabin up. Mountain weather doesn't wait, and the morning window is the one with the best chance of clear views. A short bus ride to [[shinhotaka-ropeway]], then the famous double-decker car — the only one in Japan — pulls me from 1,117m to 2,156m in two stages. The doors open onto the observation deck and the Northern Alps unfold: Yarigatake, Hotakadake, the whole serrated ridge that Japanese mountaineers spend their lives on. Late autumn snow already on the upper faces. I stay longer than planned. There's a small café up top; I have a coffee mostly as an excuse to keep looking.

Back down by lunchtime. The afternoon is for onsen hopping — the great quiet luxury of Okuhida. Each of the five villages has its own water, its own mineral profile, its own little bathhouses. I pick two or three, soak, drift, walk between them. If energy and timing allow, I take the short detour to [[hirayu-falls]] — 64 metres of cold water hitting cold rock, thirty minutes on foot from the Hirayu bus center.

Back at [[yamano-iori-hotaka-so]] before dark. Another bath before dinner — the rhythm here is bath, eat, bath, sleep. The okami serves another mountain dinner in the same small dining room. Outside, the cedars are completely black against the sky. No cars. No screens. The most still night of the trip.
