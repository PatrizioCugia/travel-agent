This is the day the trip was built around. Mid-to-late November is the only window in the year when Korankei's 4,000 maples — some of them four hundred years old — go fully red, and the gorge stays lit from 17:00 to 21:00. Everything else on this trip is excellent; this is singular.

## Morning

Early bus from [[meitetsu-bus-center]] to [[korankei-gorge]] — about 70 minutes if the road is kind, longer on a peak weekend. Arrive before the heaviest crowds. Cross [[taigetsu-bridge]] — the red arch over the Tomoe River that every photograph of Korankei eventually becomes. Walk the riverbank slowly upstream, then climb to [[kojakuji-temple]] for the panoramic view down into the gorge with the canopy on full burn.

## Lunch

[[korankei-village-lunch]] near the bridge — sansai soba or a gohei-mochi set, hot tea, hot broth, a bench in the sun.

## Afternoon

The afternoon is for not rushing. More walking upstream, smaller side trails, the trees changing again as the light drops. Don't leave for the lights — stay through the transition. Find a spot on the river by 16:30 and watch the gorge change register.

## Night

Illumination from 17:00. Cross [[taigetsu-bridge]] in the dark with the maples lit from below. Wander the [[korankei-village-stalls]] for gohei-mochi off the grill, yakitori, hot sake from a paper cup — this is the right dinner, eaten standing, cold hands wrapped around warm food.

Bus back to Nagoya late. The evening from here is deliberately unplanned — bed early if the legs are done, or one nightcap at [[gomitori]] if the city is still loud and the body still says yes. No agenda. The day already did what it came to do.
